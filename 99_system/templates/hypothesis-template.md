# Hypothesis — [service/product name]

**Opened:** [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Parent project:** [e.g. ai-services]

> This document is not a build plan — it is a falsifiable bet to test before writing anything. Fill it in with real data or explicit reasoning only; any field you are not 95% sure of stays marked as open, it is not invented.

---

## 1. Target

**ICP (ideal profile):** who needs this most desperately — a company (sector, size, revenue, team maturity) or an individual (profession, career or business stage, observable behaviours). Describe the real profile, do not force it into the "company" box if the target is a single person.

**Economic buyer:** who has the budget and authority to buy, and what they are judged on (their metrics). If the target is an individual or solopreneur, this often coincides with the ICP — in that case say so explicitly instead of inventing a distinction that does not exist.

**End user:** who will actually use the deliverable, and what they need to make their work easier. If it coincides with the economic buyer, say so instead of repeating the same profile twice.

---

## 2. Problem and Job-to-Be-Done

**JTBD:** the real objective the customer is trying to reach (not the product they buy, the outcome they want).

**Pains:** risks, obstacles, negative emotions they live with today.

**Gains:** concrete results that would make them happy.

**Existing alternatives:** how they solve the problem today — do nothing, an automated tool, a traditional agency, DIY. With names and evidence, not generically.

---

## 3. Solution and Unique Value Proposition

**Key features (max 3):** what exactly solves the pains above.

**UVP:** one sentence, end benefit, zero technical jargon.

**Unfair advantage:** what you have that is not easily copied.

---

## 4. Market and channels

**Market type:** existing market (you compete in an occupied space) or resegmentation (a new niche inside an old market)? It completely changes how you pitch.

**Acquisition channels:** where your early adopters already are, how you reach them for the first tests.

---

## 5. Pricing

**Economic value generated:** what solving this is worth to the customer (revenue, traffic, time recovered) — even a rough estimate, with the logic made explicit.

**Pricing model:** one-off, tiered, recurring — and why this one and not another.

**Early adopter price:** how much for the very first testers, and why that number.

**Competitors with real prices:** 2-3 named, with the actual price found (not guessed). If you cannot find them, say so — do not fill the field with nothing.

---

## 6. Validation — Pass/Fail

**Riskiest assumption:** the single assumption that, if false, collapses the idea.

**Pass/fail criteria:** an explicit number and time window. No "let's see how it goes" — a number that decides.

**Experiment budget:** maximum time and maximum cost before calling it failed, even if pass/fail has not yet shown itself.
